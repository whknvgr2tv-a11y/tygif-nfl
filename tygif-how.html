How it works

One rule, applied the same way every week. Records use flat $100 bets. Lines move, so this is a process, not a promise.

The money rule

Find the side with most of the money bet on it. That is where the sportsbooks have the most risk. We bet the other side. When most of the money and most of the bets are on the same team, that is Fade the Crowd.

Splits come from all sportsbooks first (ScoresAndOdds or Action Network), then DraftKings. DraftKings must have the money on the same side, or we pass.

Game types

Every game goes in exactly one type, based only on where the money is (all sportsbooks first). Checked in this order: 4, 5, 1, 2, 3.

  1. 1. Crowd piled on a big favorite: 65% or more of the money is on a favorite giving 6+ points. We take the underdog (our Big Favorite Fade).
  2. 2. Crowd piled on, no big favorite: 65% or more of the money and most of the bets are on one team, and it isn't a 6+ point favorite. We take the other team.
  3. 3. Crowd leaning: 55% to 64% of the money is on one team, or the money and the bets are on different teams. A smaller edge: LEAN at most, and a money-vs-bets split is a pass.
  4. 4. Books disagree: All sportsbooks and DraftKings have the money on different teams. No bet. Tracked from Week 5, since earlier weeks saved only one split.
  5. 5. No clear crowd: Money near even: under 55% on either team. No bet.

Injuries and line moves never change the type. They only drop the rating one level inside it.

Ratings

STRONG = Fade the Crowd and 65%+ of the money on the all-sportsbooks split, with the DraftKings split agreeing on the side. It is our top rating. LEAN is a smaller edge (money under 65%) or a STRONG that dropped a level. LEANs are optional. PASS means no bet.

What drops a play one level

  • The line moved more than 1 point toward the money.
  • Mixed signals between sources.
  • Our usual starting QB (the team's named QB1 that week) is out, doubtful or questionable on the official injury report (Weeks 1–4, with no saved reports: he didn't start).

When we pass

  • Big bettors vs the public: the money is on one team but most bets are on the other. Betting against that lost early, so we track it and don't bet.
  • Game types 4 and 5: the books disagree, or there is no clear crowd.

How records are kept

Every rated play is graded at flat $100, settled at the DraftKings closing price. Wins, losses and pushes all stay on the record. The Weeks 1–4 numbers are a look back: today's rules applied afterward to finished games. The live record starts with Week 5 and is kept separate.

Any Dallas game gets a heads-up star. The call still comes from the money, not the team.

Data notes and methods

Money and lines

Profit convention: flat $100 risked per bet. Card plays, ratings, signals and Money Rule records are settled at the actual DraftKings closing spread price (so a win pays +$83 to +$102, not always +$91); market, trend and situational tables are settled at -110 (win = +$90.91). Loss = -$100, push = $0. Cover % excludes pushes. straight-up win % counts ties as half a win. Lines: DraftKings closing spread via ESPN odds API.

How our ratings would have done

These are re-runs, not live results: today's card rules applied to every final game (weeks 1–4) using the closing betting splits. DraftKings' own splits have no history before Oct 5, so DraftKings-based downgrades can't happen here. QB injury status wasn't saved for those weeks, so the QB drop uses who actually started: if our side's usual starter (the team's named QB1 that week) didn't start, it counts as out and drops one level. They are the same games the rules were tuned on, so treat this as a best case. It never changes the card rules.

When big bettors and the public split

Sharps split = money majority on one side, bet majority on the other. Upper table: every final game, all-books closing consensus. The smaller tables come from our own card: games we tracked but didn't bet, settled both ways.

Bet against the money

Standing rule: always against the side holding most of the money (books' exposure). Bet % is context only. 'Fading the public' = money and bets on the same side; 'Fading the sharps' = money on one side, bets on the other. Big favorite angle = favorite 6+ AND money on the favorite -> play the dog vs spread. Splits: all-books closing consensus for every Weeks 1–4 game.

Trends we're watching

A split is flagged the first time it shows 10+ decided games and cover % ≥ 60% or ≤ 40%. Flag date/time and the record at flag are frozen on the day it was flagged and never reset. 'Since flagged' counts ONLY final games kicking off after the flag timestamp. Lean $ = $100/bet at -110 on the lean side. Exact mirror splits (e.g. Home underdogs = Road favorites) are tracked once, under the favorite split. Retired = the lean went 40% or worse over 8+ decided games since it was flagged (kept on the list, no longer used on cards).

All splits

Record side = whose straight-up/vs spread record is shown (Fav or Dog). Profit = $100/bet at -110 on the record side (win +$90.91, loss -$100, push $0). Cover % excludes pushes. won-no-cover = favorite won but did not cover (pushes are not won-no-cover); won-no-cover % = won-no-cover / favorite straight-up wins. Sample warning: under 15 games. Green = cover % ≥ 60%, red ≤ 40%. Lean on watchlist = 10+ decided games and cover % ≥ 60% or ≤ 40%.

Public vs the books

Splits source: ScoresAndOdds consensus (Action Network data) - bet % and money % as published (final displayed values; the source gives no freeze time, treat as ~closing). This is an aggregate 'public' measure, not one sportsbook's money. Spread angles are settled at the DraftKings close (same line as the rest of the tracker). Public side = more than 50% of bets (50/50 excluded). Reverse game = money % majority on the side with fewer bets. Profit = $100/bet at -110 on the described side. Win % excludes pushes. p = two-sided exact binomial test vs 50%. Sample warning: under 15 games or p ≥ 0.05. Non-final games, and final games whose splits were last pulled pre-game (not refreshed), are excluded.

Coverage: 64 of 64 final games have spread splits; 63 have a public side and a favorite (spread angles).

Caveat: tiny samples from one season; percentages are an aggregate published measure and their exact freeze time is not given. Not a validated edge.
