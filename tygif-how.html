How it works

One rule, applied the same way to every game. Spread bets only. Records use flat $100 bets.

The rule

Find the side holding most of the money. That is where the sportsbooks have the most risk. We bet the other side.

Splits come from all sportsbooks first (ScoresAndOdds), then DraftKings. DraftKings must have the money on the same side, or we pass.

Five game types

Every game goes in exactly one type, based only on where the money is. Checked in this order: 4, 5, 1, 2, 3.

  1. Crowd piled on a big favorite: 65%+ of the money is on a favorite giving 6+ points. We take the underdog.
  2. Crowd piled on, no big favorite: 65%+ of the money and most of the bets are on one team that isn’t a 6+ point favorite. We take the other team.
  3. Crowd leaning: 55–64% of the money is on one team, or the money and bets are on different teams. LEAN at most, and a money-vs-bets split is a pass.
  4. Books disagree: all sportsbooks and DraftKings have the money on different teams. No bet.
  5. No clear crowd: under 55% of the money on either team. No bet.

Injuries and line moves never change the type. They only drop the rating one level inside it.

Six checks

  1. Money and bets on the same side. If not, PASS.
  2. 65%+ of the money (all books). Yes starts at STRONG; 55–64% starts at LEAN.
  3. DraftKings has the money on the same side. If not, it is type 4: PASS.
  4. A 6+ point favorite holds the money. Sets type 1 instead of type 2. Doesn’t change the rating.
  5. The line hasn’t moved over 1 point toward the money from the start-of-week line (Sunday night, before Monday) to the close. If it has, drop one level.
    How the start-of-week line is setTeamRankings books 2 and 3, each book’s last quote before Monday 12:00 AM ET, averaged. Quotes older than 7 days are skipped; if only one book has a quote, that book is used.
  6. Our QB1 is playing. If he is out, doubtful or questionable on the official injury report, or on IR, drop one level. QB1 = the team’s starter when healthy (its Week 1 starter, or its named starter if Week 1 was a fill-in). Weeks 1–4, with no saved reports: he didn’t start.

Mixed signals: when the sources disagree (all books and DraftKings have the money on different teams, type 4) or the money and the bets are on different teams, the game is a PASS.

STRONG, LEAN, PASS

STRONG · top ratingLEAN · optionalPASS · no bet
  • STRONG: money and bets on the same side (all books), 65%+ of the money (all books), DraftKings money on the same side (DraftKings bets don’t need to agree), nothing drops it.
  • LEAN: 55–64% of the money, or a STRONG that dropped one level.
  • PASS: types 4 and 5, money and bets on different sides, or a LEAN that dropped.

STRONG and LEAN records are always kept separate, never added together.

How records are kept

  • Every STRONG and LEAN play is graded at flat $100, counted from our side. A loss is −$100 and a push is $0. Wins, losses and pushes all stay on the record.
  • Weeks 1–4 are a look back: today’s rules applied afterward to the same 64 games used to set them. Small samples; treat them as a best case.
  • Calls posted before kickoff are previews and can change as the money moves. Every week is rated and graded at the closing numbers: the DraftKings closing line and price, and the money splits (all books and DraftKings) from the last pull before kickoff.
  • The live log (data/live_log.csv) saves each play’s closing line, price and splits, which it is graded on, and the posted call (line, price, time) as history.

Sources

ScoresAndOdds (all-sportsbook money and bet splits) · DraftKings (its own splits and lines) · TeamRankings (start-of-week line) · ESPN (scores and closing lines) · nflverse (Weeks 1–4 starting quarterbacks) · team injury reports.
