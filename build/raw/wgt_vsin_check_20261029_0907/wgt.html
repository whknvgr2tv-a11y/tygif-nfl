How do you read NFL betting splits?

Definition. NFL betting splits are the percentage of bets and of dollars on each side of an NFL market.

Worked example. Team B +3 has 35 percent of tickets and 61 percent of money, a 26 point gap. Team A has 65 percent of tickets but 39 percent of money. The money sits on B even though most tickets are on A.

Exchange buyers: which side buyers took (Kalshi, Polymarket, ProphetX)

Exchange buyers is not a betting split. On an exchange every trade has a buyer and a seller, so the money is always even. This shows which side the buyers who paid the asking price took. Kalshi and Polymarket publish every trade with the side the buyer took. Trades is each side's share of trades since the market opened, Money its share of the dollars buyers paid, $ traded the total paid (with the last 24 hours in brackets) and $ open the contracts still open (each pays $1).

ProphetX and Novig publish no live trade sides, so they show volume and prices instead: best price is the best odds a buyer can take on that side right now, and $ at price the money on offer at those odds. ProphetX $ matched covers every line of the market, because ProphetX publishes no figure per line.

Novig counts contracts that each pay $1. Spread and total rows show the line nearest the sportsbook consensus and are left out when an exchange has no line within 1.5 points (spread) or 2 points (total). These are exchange trades, not sportsbook bets. Markets with fewer than 10 trades or under $500 traded are left out.

Markets with fewer than 30 trades or under $5,000 traded are shown in a lighter style with a Light trading label, because with only a few trades one or two buyers can move the percentages a long way. ProphetX and Novig publish no trade count, so the same dollar levels apply to $ matched and contracts traded. An exchange row last read more than 45 minutes ago shows the time it is from in the lighter style, and one older than 3 hours is left out.

How to read this board

1Bets % vs Money %Bets % is the share of tickets (the number of bets) on a side. Money % is the share of dollars (the handle) on it. Both come from the reported sample for that market, so they describe the market, not any one bettor.
2Gap (pts)Money % minus Bets %, in percentage points. Example: 40% of the bets but 58% of the money is a gap of +18 points, which means the average wager on that side is larger than on the other side. A gap near zero means tickets and dollars agree.
3Sharp 🔪 (sharp side)The knife lights on the side with the biggest money-over-bets gap when that gap is 8 points or more, and the line under the game calls it the sharp side. Fewer bettors are putting up more of the money there, the pattern most people call sharp action. It is a pattern in this sample, not a win probability, not proof of who bet, and not proof that a line moved because of it.
4Big $ 💰 (big money)A side holding 65% or more of the money with a gap of at least 5 points over its share of bets. Favorites often carry most of the money on their own, so the gap requirement keeps this flag honest.

How to read NFL bets versus money

Start with the market and line, then compare Bets % with Money %. Bets % is the share of reported tickets on that side. Money % is the share of reported dollars. A gap can show a difference in average wager size within the sample, but it cannot tell you who placed those wagers.

Example only: a team with 40% of tickets and 65% of money has a positive 25 percentage-point gap. That is a description of the sample, not a predicted win rate. Check the current price and any available line history before drawing a conclusion.

NFL spread, total and moneyline markets

The spread applies a points handicap to each team. At -3.5, a team must win by at least four points to cover; at +3.5, it covers by winning or losing by three or fewer. A total compares combined points with the posted over/under line. A moneyline is a wager on the game winner. Read each market's own percentages and price, since they can differ within the same matchup.

NFL consensus: tickets vs money

Consensus describes the side with the largest share of reported tickets in a selected market. Tickets count wagers, not individual bettors. Money % describes the share of reported dollars. Neither percentage identifies the people placing those bets.

Gap is money share minus ticket share, measured in percentage points. The sharp-action flag (the knife) lights at a gap of 8 points or more, meaning fewer tickets are carrying more of the money on that side. The big-money flag lights when a side holds 65% or more of the money with a gap of at least 5 points. Compare the same market and source snapshot. A flag is a pattern in the reported sample, not a win probability and not proof of who placed the bets. Explore public betting percentages across sports or read about recorded line movement.

Where this data comes from, and its limits

Source. Splits are licensed from a third-party market-data feed that samples betting activity across regulated US sportsbooks. Percentages represent that tracked sample, not every dollar bet in America, and different books can show different mixes. Odds are compared across the regulated books we track, and the timestamp on the board shows the last refresh.

How to read it. A gap between ticket share and money share describes the distribution of wagers in this sample. It does not identify the bettors or establish the cause of a price change. Frequently asked questions

What are Circa betting splits?

Circa betting splits are the share of bets and the share of money on each side at Circa Sports, the Las Vegas book known for taking the largest limits from sharp bettors. When Circa's money leans one way while most DraftKings tickets are on the other side, this page calls it a sharp vs public split.

Do book splits differ from the market?

Often, yes. DraftKings splits describe DraftKings customers, a large recreational audience, so they can lean further toward favorites and overs than other books. Comparing them with Circa's money, as this page does for every game, shows where the public and the sharper money disagree.

What is NFL public betting?

Public betting describes the distribution of reported tickets on the sides of a market. Ticket share counts wagers, not unique people. Money share describes the reported dollars.

What are NFL public betting percentages?

They show the share of reported betting tickets on each side of a NFL market. Money percentages show the share of reported dollars. Both describe the source's sample.

Does a higher money percentage prove professional betting?

No. It can indicate a larger average wager on that side within the sample. The percentages do not identify the bettors.

Why are some NFL percentages unavailable?

Our source may have odds for a game without publishing ticket or money percentages for that market. Those games show lines and best prices only, and a market that has Bets % but no Money % shows Bets % alone, until the source supplies the rest.

Are betting splits the same as reverse line movement?

No. Splits describe tickets and money at a point in time. Reverse line movement compares a recorded price change with the distribution of tickets. One snapshot cannot establish that movement or its cause.

How often does the board update?

The page retrieves the current board when opened and when you select Refresh. Board retrieval and source ingestion are labeled separately. A recent retrieval does not mean the source has supplied new percentages.

What is the betting handle?

Handle is the total amount of money wagered on a game or market. On this board, Money % is the share of the reported handle on each side, and Bets % is the share of tickets. When a side holds a bigger share of the handle than of the tickets, the average wager on it is larger within the sample; it does not tell you who placed the bets.
