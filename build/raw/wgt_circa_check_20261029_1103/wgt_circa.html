How to read Circa betting splits

Two numbers matter on every row: the share of bets (tickets) and the share of money (handle) on each side. When the money share runs well ahead of the ticket share, the average wager on that side is larger in Circa's sample. That is a reason to look closer, not a reason to bet, and it does not identify who placed the bets.

Circa vs DraftKings: two different crowds

DraftKings publishes figures for a huge national app audience. Circa's figures come from one Las Vegas book with a smaller, different customer base. When the two lean opposite ways on the same game, that gap is worth knowing. See DraftKings betting splits for DraftKings' own figures, and every sport's market splits on the betting splits hub.

Circa's line vs the best price

Circa prices for its own book. The Best ML column names the regulated US book we track with the strongest current price, so you can compare Circa's number with one you can actually bet where you are. Our free line-shopping tool runs the comparison across every market.

Are these Circa's own numbers?

Yes. The bets and money percentages on this page are the figures Circa publishes for each game, read through the day and shown with the time they were read. They describe Circa's customers, not the whole market, and they are not house liability, which no sportsbook publishes. When Circa has no real figure for a game, that game says so instead of showing a number.

Where this data comes from, and its limits

Prices: Circa's moneyline is the price Circa publishes with its splits; the best line comes from a multi-book odds feed of the regulated US books we track, retrieved when you open the page. Splits: the bets% and money% figures are Circa's own published betting percentages, read every few minutes and shown with the time they were read. They describe Circa's customers, not every bet placed. Prices move constantly; confirm the current number before betting.

Circa betting splits: frequently asked questions

What are Circa betting splits?

Betting splits show the share of bets (tickets) and the share of money (handle) on each side of a game. On this page they are Circa Sports' own published percentages, shown next to Circa's posted moneyline and the best price we track.

Why do people watch Circa's splits?

Circa is a Las Vegas book known for taking larger wagers than most apps, so a side where Circa's money share runs well ahead of its ticket share is a different read from the national apps. It describes Circa's customers only and it does not identify who placed the bets.

How do Circa's splits differ from DraftKings' splits?

DraftKings publishes figures for a large national app audience; Circa's come from a smaller Las Vegas book. When the two lean opposite ways on the same game, that gap is worth a closer look. Our DraftKings betting splits page shows DraftKings' own figures the same way.

Can I bet at Circa from anywhere?

No. Circa takes bets only where it is licensed. The best-price column names a regulated US book we track, so you can compare Circa's number with what you can actually bet where you are.

Why does a game say no Circa numbers?

Circa has not posted a real bets and money figure for that game yet, or the figure we read is older than our freshness limit. We never fill in a number Circa did not publish.

How often do these splits update?

Circa's percentages are read every few minutes on game days. Each figure is as of the time shown and can change within minutes, so confirm the current price before betting.
