BetMGM Sportsbook Public Betting Percentages

How to Read Public Betting Trends & Percentages

You can see where the bets are going at the BetMGM Sportsbook. Track real-time public betting trends for the percentage of bets (straight and parlays) across the NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, and other sports.

Spread Bet %
(Percentage of Bets)

Shows the popularity of each side of a spread for the game. For example, if the Chicago Cubs have 75% of the spread (or run line) bets against the St. Louis Cardinals, 75% of all bets placed (on either side of the spread) are on the Cubs to cover. The remaining 25% of tickets are on the Cardinals to cover.

Total Bet %
(Percentage of Bets)

Shows the popularity of each side of an over/under total for the game. For example, the Over has 90% of the Over/Under Total bets in a game between the Detroit Lions and Philadelphia Eagles, 90% of all bets placed (on either side of the Over/Under Total). The remaining 10% is on the Under.

Moneyline Bet %
(Percentage of Bets)

Shows the popularity of each moneyline for the game. For example, if the New York Knicks have 62% of the moneyline bets against the Boston Celtics, 62% of all moneyline bets placed (on either team) are on the Knicks to win the game. The remaining 38% is on the Celtics.

Using Public Betting Splits for Betting Strategy

BetMGM bettors could use public splits — if available — as part of their betting strategy. Here’s a look at how the splits may be used and/or interpreted:

Fading the Public

To fade the public, you place a bet on the side with the overwhelming minority of bets.

For example: If 89% of the bets are on the Kansas City Chiefs’ spread (-4.5) against the Denver Broncos, the public is overwhelmingly favoring the Chiefs to cover 4.5 points (i.e., win by at least five points). Betting on the Broncos — +4.5 with only 11% of the bets would be fading the public.

Bettors could, for example, compare public betting splits to trends to determine if one team has been terrible against the spread this season but remains a popular pick. That could present betting value.

Bettors can also use BetMGM’s Odds Calculator to calculate implied probability and compare against what value they believe odds to have.

Line Movement

When odds change for a betting market, it’s called line movement.

For example: The Carolina Hurricanes opened as -250 moneyline favorites against the Pittsburgh Penguins. If the Hurricanes’ moneyline odds change to -200 prior to the start of the game, the moneyline moved.

Bettors could compare line movement to public betting splits to determine if, potentially, the line shifted as a result of increased betting interest on one side of a market.

In the example above, if the Hurricanes had only 10% of the moneyline bets prior to the line movement from -250 to -200 but entered the game with 50% of the bets — a 40-point movement in ticket share — bettors may interpret the movement as a result of the previously low interest in the Hurricanes’ moneyline.

FAQs: Public Betting Data

What Are Public Betting Splits?

Public betting splits show the percentage of bets on each side of a betting market. It’s the distribution of the volume of bets made by all BetMGM customers for that market.

What is Fading the Public?

Fading the public is a strategy when a bet is placed against the overwhelming majority of bettors.

What is Line Movement?

Line movement is the adjustment of spreads, totals, moneylines, and other odds by the BetMGM Sportsbook prior to the event start.

What is Reverse Line Movement?

Reverse line movement happens when odds move in the opposite direction of the public percentage. 

Can Public Betting Slips Change?

Yes, public betting splits can move from the time the betting markets open until the time the markets close. Splits change as bets are placed and the data is updated.

Betting Markets

If you bet on the Los Angeles Rams spread -3.5 against the Seattle Seahawks, the Rams must win the game by at least four points to cover the spread. If you bet on the Seahawks spread +3.5, the Seahawks must lose the game by three points or fewer (or win the game). If the spread is a whole number that is the exact margin of victory, the bet is a push and your money is refunded.
