How to read NFL betting splits

Ticket percentage counts bets. It is a headcount, so it is dominated by recreational bettors backing favorites, primetime brands and overs. Money percentage (handle) weights those bets by size, so a handful of five-figure wagers can swing it dramatically.

A split like 72% of tickets but 38% of the money on a favorite means the public is piling on while larger accounts take the other side. The NFL is the most liquid betting market in the United States, so genuine divergences are rarer — and more valuable — than in college football.

Pair the split with the line. If the number moves against the ticket majority, that is reverse line movement — books shading toward the money they respect. Those games are flagged on the sharp money tracker.

FAQ

NFL betting splits FAQ

What are NFL betting splits?+

NFL betting splits show the share of tickets (number of bets) and the share of handle (money wagered) on each side of a game. When the two diverge, it often signals professional money against the public.

What is the difference between ticket percentage and money percentage?+

Ticket percentage counts every bet equally. Money percentage weights each bet by its size, so a few large wagers can swing the number far from the ticket count.

What is reverse line movement in the NFL?+

Reverse line movement happens when the point spread or total moves toward the side receiving fewer bets. Sportsbooks shade the line because respected money is forcing the market.
