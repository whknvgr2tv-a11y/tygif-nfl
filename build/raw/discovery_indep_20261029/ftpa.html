About NFL public betting percentages

We track the percentage of bets and the percentage of dollars on each side of every NFL game — spread, total and moneyline — updated continuously from the moment a line is posted through kickoff. When those two numbers diverge — one side has most of the tickets but the other side has most of the money — the dollars usually carry the sharper signal. The games at the top of this page are ranked by the strength of that split.

The NFL board is the most heavily bet in American sports, so a lopsided ticket count on a name-brand team is normal here; the number that matters is where the money lands against it. We don't issue picks on this page. Use the splits to inform your own read of the game, and bet responsibly.

Frequently asked questions

What are NFL public betting percentages?

The percentage of bets (tickets) and the percentage of money (handle) on each side of an NFL market — spread, total and moneyline. Bet percentage tells you what the crowd is doing; money percentage tells you where the dollars are. When the two disagree, the larger, sharper bets are usually on the money side.

Where does this NFL betting data come from?

From publicly reported sportsbook handle, captured on a sub-hourly cadence from the moment a game is listed through kickoff. Every game on this page shows its latest published split; the paid NFL analytics page also stores the full history so line and money movement can be tracked.

How do you use NFL betting splits to fade the public?

Look for games where one side has a large majority of the bets but a much smaller share of the money. That gap means the average bet on the other side is bigger, which is the footprint of sharper action. The games at the top of this page are ranked by the size of that gap on the spread, the market NFL bettors trade most.

Is this NFL public betting data free?

Yes. The splits on this page are free and refresh continuously through the game week. The subscriber page adds expected-value plays, reverse line movement and a graded season record on top of the same data.
